⚖️ Key Legal Issues

JURISDICTION · SUBSTANTIVE RIGHTS · PROCEDURE

A non-exhaustive compilation of principal disputing issues arising in China-related Investor–State Dispute Settlement (ISDS) cases.

?

Treaty Interpretation of Limited Jurisdiction Clauses under Earlier Chinese IIAs

Analysis of how limited jurisdiction clauses in earlier Chinese International Investment Agreements (IIAs) have been interpreted and applied in ISDS proceedings, examining the scope and boundaries of arbitral tribunals' jurisdiction.

Example: Article 8.3, China-Peru BIT (1994):

"If a dispute involving the amount of compensation for expropriation cannot be settled within six months after resort to negotiations as specified in Paragraph 1 of this Article, it may be submitted at the request of either party to the international arbitration of the International Center for Settlement of Investment Disputes (ICSID)…."
?

The Applicability of Chinese IIAs to Hong Kong SAR and Macau SAR

Examination of the application and interpretation of Chinese investment treaties to the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region and Macau Special Administrative Region, including jurisdictional questions and treaty coverage.

?

The Identity of Chinese SOEs as Qualified Investors

Analysis of the qualification of Chinese State-Owned Enterprises (SOEs) as investors under international investment agreements, examining the criteria for investor status, the relationship between SOEs and the State, and the implications for ISDS jurisdiction and substantive protection.

?

The Dual Nationality of Investors

Examination of how dual (or multiple) nationality affects standing and admissibility in ISDS, including the application of dominant and effective nationality tests, treaty-based definitions of “investor,” and potential conflicts between home and host state nationality links.

?

Assessment of Host-State Measures as Expropriation

Analysis of how tribunals assess alleged expropriation by the host state, including direct and indirect takings, the role of police powers, standards for substantial deprivation and interference, and the distinction between lawful and unlawful expropriation.

?

The Application of Exception Clauses

Examination of how exception clauses (e.g., security, public order, health, environmental measures) are interpreted and applied in ISDS, including thresholds for invocation, burden of proof, and the relationship between exceptions, necessity defenses, and proportionality review.
